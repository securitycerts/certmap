Why certifications still matter

Employers still name certifications in job adverts, still use them to shortlist junior candidates and still pay for their staff to earn them. These figures come from the reports themselves; each links to its source.

The counterweight: most professionals got into security through hands-on IT work (56%) rather than through a certification (6%), according to the 2025 ISC2 Workforce Study. A certification helps open the door and practice backs it up, which is why every study plan here ends in hands-on labs.

What it is

The roadmap places 493 certifications from 93 vendors on a grid of 17 domains and four seniority levels, from entry-level credentials such as CompTIA Security+ to expert ones such as OSCE3 and CISSP. Each entry lists the current exam price in US dollars, the length and format of the exam, the renewal cycle and the prerequisites, checked against the vendor's own page.

Pick the certifications you are aiming for and the site orders them by prerequisite, adds up the exam cost and the study time, and gives you a link to share the result. The project is open source, the dataset is free to reuse and corrections are welcome.

How to use it

  1. ExploreScan the matrix by domain and level, or filter by vendor, price, exam format and DoD 8140 status.
  2. SelectAdd the certifications you want. Prerequisites can be pulled in with one click.
  3. PlanSee the path in order, with the total exam cost and an estimate of the study time.
  4. StudyFollow each certification's study plan: exam guide, courses, free labs and practice questions, numbered in order.
  5. ShareName the roadmap and copy its link. Anyone who opens it sees the same path.

Independent by design

Questions and answers

What is a cybersecurity certification roadmap?

A roadmap lays certifications out by subject and by seniority so you can see which ones lead to which. cert/map places 493 certifications from 93 vendors on a grid of 17 domains and four levels, from beginner to expert, and shows the price, exam format and prerequisites of each.

Which cybersecurity certification should a beginner start with?

The most widely recognised entry-level certifications on the roadmap are Security+ ($439), CC ($199), A+ ($274), ACE ($125), AZ-900 ($99). Which one fits depends on the direction you want to take: general security, networking, cloud or hands-on testing.

How much do cybersecurity certifications cost?

Exam prices on the roadmap run from free to $4,495. The cheapest paid exams are CRT-COI ($9), CEDP ($29), CELMS ($29), and the most expensive are NACD CERT ($4,495), CCSFP ($3,300), BTL2 ($2,623). Prices are list prices for the exam, without training unless the vendor only sells them together, shown in the currency the vendor uses and in US dollars.

Can I see certification prices in my own currency?

Yes. Prices stay as the vendor publishes them, in US dollars, pounds or euros, and the interactive roadmap can show each one, and the total of your roadmap, in 31 currencies. Conversions use the European Central Bank reference rates, refreshed every working day. Where card payments abroad carry a tax, the rate includes it, so the figure is close to what a card is actually charged.

Are there free cybersecurity certifications?

Yes. Basel OSINT, CTI 101, OSCE3 have no exam fee.

Which certifications have a hands-on exam?

201 of the 493 certifications are assessed with a practical exam in a lab rather than multiple choice, among them Cisco EH, MCRTA, OSCC-SEC, PJPT, CCO, CDSA.

What is DoD 8140 and which certifications count for it?

DoD 8140 is the US Department of Defense framework for qualifying everyone who does cyber work for it, contractors included, and it replaced the older 8570 programme. Each person has to reach a foundational qualification for their work role within nine months, and an approved certification is one way to do it, alongside education, training or experience. 49 certifications on the roadmap appear in the DoD 8140 qualification matrix, including A+, CC, GFACT, Linux+, Network+, Security+.

How current are the prices?

382 of 493 prices carry the date they were last checked against the vendor's own page; the most recent check was on 2026-11-26. Each certification page shows its own date. Vendors change prices without notice, so confirm before paying.

Does cert/map help me study for a certification?

Yes. 79 certifications have a study plan: the exam guide first, then courses and reading, then hands-on practice with direct links to free labs and open-source projects, then practice questions. The plans hold 655 links in total, and every link was opened when it was added.

Can I build and share my own certification path?

Yes. Select certifications in the interactive roadmap and it orders them by prerequisite and adds up exam cost and study time. Give the path a name, pick your currency and copy its link: anyone who opens it sees the same roadmap with the same currency. It can also be copied as Markdown or downloaded as CSV with the converted prices.

For developers

A free JSON API, no key needed Every certification with its price, level, prerequisites, study plan and comparisons, as static JSON you can fetch from any site. One file per certification, for example /api/v1/certs/oscp.json.

Data and licence

The whole dataset is one CSV file, also published as JSON, with a plain-text digest for language models at llms.txt and llms-full.txt. The code is MIT licensed and the dataset is CC BY 4.0. Corrections are welcome on GitHub.
